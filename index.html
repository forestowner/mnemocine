Bisociation is perceiving something in two habitually incompatible frames of reference at once — Arthur Koestler's term (The Act of Creation, 1964) for the collision he thought underlies humour, discovery, and art. Mnemocine deals three images from the open archives side by side so you can practise it: take references that don't belong together and find what binds them. A palette, a gesture, a story, an art direction.

The name joins Mnemosyne — Greek memory, mother of the Muses, and the title of Aby Warburg's Bilderatlas (1924–29), whose panels of pinned photographs made their meaning in the intervals between images — with cine, cinema in Spanish. Memory dealt as frames.

No account, no subscription, no tracking. Inspired by @russellrjones94's Random Image Selector, which shuffles a folder on your own computer; this is the web cousin, drawing on the archives instead. Downloads embed only what each archive permits — the Met and Cleveland block it, so those panels export as linked cards.
